ReputableChat rules, version 0.001

These rules define each record, its fields, and the rules for validating it. This document is the rules field of the genesis record, which is the developer's identity declaration. Whenever the rules change, a release record carrying the new rules is added to the chain, and every later record must conform to it. These rules distinguish between rules that are enforced and guidelines which are not. A guideline is a suggestion: a record that ignores one is still accepted, but clients will likely ignore the nonconforming values.

1. RECORDS

A record is a payload and a signature over it.

Payload
  A JSON object in canonical form: keys sorted, no whitespace, UTF-8, and floating-point numbers recorded as a string surrounded by double quotes.

Signature
  Ed25519 over the canonical bytes of the payload, made by the key in the payload's pubkey or mpubkey field, written as base64url (64 bytes). A record carrying both is valid if the signature verifies against either.

Record hash
  SHA-256 over the canonical payload, a newline, and the signature, written as 64 lowercase hex characters. It is the record's identity. Canonical JSON and base64url never contain a raw newline, and base64url is written without padding.

Text
  UTF-8 with surrounding whitespace removed. It contains no control characters other than tab, newline and carriage return. Each field gives its own length limit, counted in bytes.

Decimal
  A number written as a string: an optional minus sign, a whole part with no leading zeros, and optionally a point followed by one to eighteen digits, the last of which is not zero, such as "0.5" or "-1". Zero is written "0", never "-0". Each number has exactly one spelling.

File
  Files are too big to include on the chain, so a record refers to one by the SHA-256 of its bytes in hex plus a file extension. The chain does not verify whether the file can be found, whether it matches its extension, or whether the extension is a known one; it only verifies that the extension is 1 to 5 lowercase letters or digits.

Validity
  Whether a record is valid is decided by the record and the history it acknowledges, and by nothing else. So its author can calculate it before signing, every reader reaches the same answer, and no record that arrives later can make an earlier one invalid.

2. COMMON FIELDS

Unless otherwise stated each field is required. Some optional fields may be required by certain record types.

Record type (type)
  Three or more parts separated by colons. The first is always "reputablechat". The second is one of: attestation, identity, message, notice, reaction or release. The third is the rules version the record conforms to. Any further parts are ignored by the chain. The whole type is at most 256 bytes. Records must conform to the newest rules they acknowledge; a record whose version does not match those rules is invalid.

  Attestation: A public statement of how reliable other users are. Example: reputablechat:attestation:v0.001
  Identity: A public declaration of identity, including a preferred handle and the account's keys. Example: reputablechat:identity:v0.001
  Message: A message to be displayed on the network. Example: reputablechat:message:v0.001
  Notice: An alert posted to the network, which may or may not be displayed to users. Example: reputablechat:notice:v0.001
  Reaction: A reaction to another record on the network, such as a like. Example: reputablechat:reaction:v0.001
  Release: A published version of the client. Example: reputablechat:release:v0.001

Account ID (id)
  The record hash of an account's first identity declaration. It is used to identify the account. This field is omitted only in the first identity declaration.

Working key (pubkey)
  Optional, but required when the record is signed with the account's working key, and on every first identity declaration, which declares it. The working key is the key an account uses day to day, as opposed to its master key: an Ed25519 public key written as base64url (32 bytes). A record carries pubkey, mpubkey, or both, and is signed with one of the keys it carries. A record carrying neither is invalid.

  An account's current keys, as seen by a record, are the working key and master public key set by its first identity declaration, as replaced by the latest key-change and master-key-change notices that the record acknowledges, directly or through the records it acknowledges. A record whose pubkey, or mpubkey if present, does not match the account's current keys as seen by that record is invalid and rejected. A record that was valid stays valid after the account changes keys.

Ack (ack)
  The record hashes of the most recent records the author had seen and chose to acknowledge: sorted, with no duplicates, at most 16. Empty in the genesis record and nowhere else. Every record it names must share this record's version, unless this is a release record. Following acks back from any record always leads to the genesis record.

Body (body)
  Text, at most 16,000 bytes, and may be empty. It can carry a bio, or the text of a message. The chain checks only that it is Text, so it can equally carry structured data, such as a JSON object holding a bio alongside other details.

Rules (rules)
  Text, 1 to 64,000 bytes: a complete rules document, such as this one. Required on the genesis record and on every release record, and not allowed on any other record.

Record timestamp (ts)
  The time the author says they signed, in whole seconds since 1970-01-01 00:00 UTC. It is not verified, and the network largely ignores it.

Target (target)
  Optional. A list of record hashes of existing records on the chain that this record replies to, comments on or reacts to: sorted, with no duplicates, at most 256.

Endorse (endorse)
  Optional. A list of record hashes the author has examined and accepts: sorted, with no duplicates, at most 256. It is not an ack: it says nothing about what the author had seen and does not put the named records in this record's history. The chain acts on it only where these rules say so, and ignores it everywhere else.

Transfer (transfer)
  Optional. Moves currency, as set out in section 9. An object holding out, and optionally currency and in:

  currency: the account ID of the currency being moved. Omitted when it is the author's own.
  in: the outputs this record spends, each written as the record hash of the record that made it, a colon, and the output's index in that record's out list, counting from 0 with no leading zeros: sorted, with no duplicates, at most 256. Omitted when the record spends nothing.
  out: the outputs this record makes, in the author's order, 1 to 256 of them. Each holds value, a decimal greater than zero, and to, the account ID it goes to. An output without to destroys its value.

Title (title)
  Optional. Text, 1 to 280 bytes: a short line naming or summing up the record, such as a forum post's title.

File (file)
  Optional. A list of files, as defined above, that the record refers to: in the author's order, with no duplicates, at most 16.

Url (url)
  Optional. Text, 1 to 2,048 bytes, containing no whitespace. The network does not check that it is a URL or restrict its scheme.

  Guideline: a URI such as "https://example.com/" or "ftp://example.com/file". Clients decide which schemes they make clickable, show the rest as plain text, and never fetch a url on a reader's behalf.

Language (lang)
  Optional. Text, 1 to 35 bytes of letters, digits and hyphens: the language the record is written in.

  Guideline: a BCP 47 language tag, such as "en" or "pt-BR".

Master public key (mpubkey)
  Optional, but required when the record is signed with the master key. Users will sometimes want to change their key for security, or need to recover their account after a key is compromised. So a master key should be created with the identity declaration and kept offline, to be used if the working key is compromised. A master public key is an Ed25519 public key written as base64url (32 bytes). A first identity declaration that carries it declares the account's master key, and may be signed with either key. The master public key can only be changed by a master-key-change notice signed with the current master key.

3. IDENTITY DECLARATION RECORDS

An identity declaration requires title, which holds the handle, and may also carry an avatar, a bio and a list of adjudicators.

Handle (title)
  The network identifies each user by their account ID, which is neither pleasant to look at nor easy to read. So each user chooses a handle to be known by, 1 to 64 bytes. Handles are not unique: the first few characters of the account ID are shown beside a handle, as a kind of last name.

Avatar (file)
  The first file in the list: an image, named by the SHA-256 of its bytes in hex plus a file extension. Images are expected to be 128 x 128 with a common image extension. The network does not enforce either, but most servers will likely ignore images that do not conform.

Bio (body)
  Text about the user. The network limits it only as it limits any body; each app decides how much of it to show.

Adjudicators (adjudicators)
  Optional. A list of account IDs, in the author's order, with no duplicates, at most 16. They are the accounts this account appoints to settle which key change it accepts, should it ever be compromised. An absent or empty list names the developer's account alone.

  This is the one field a new identity declaration does not change by itself. A declaration carrying a different list takes effect in every other field at once, while the list last endorsed stays in force until a quorum record names the new one. Any key that can sign for the account could otherwise appoint the accounts that would later decide who owns it.

  Guideline: an adjudicator is expected to endorse regularly, so that an account is never left compromised for want of an answer, and to wait long enough before endorsing a key change that the account's owner has a fair chance to notice and dispute it. How long that is, and whether to endorse by hand or after a quiet interval, is each adjudicator's own business; an account may name several, and drop one that answers too slowly or too fast.

The genesis record is the one identity declaration that carries rules. Its body is the genesis account's bio, like anyone else's.

4. ATTESTATION RECORDS

An attestation requires one additional field, scores, and may also carry derived.

Scores (scores)
  A map from each rated user's account ID to the author's opinion of them. Each value holds reputation and trust, both decimals from -1 to +1. Reputation is the author's opinion of the user's messages; trust is the author's opinion of the user's attestations. Trust is expected to be 0 or 1 in most cases. A 0 marks a user who is honest but too trusting of bad actors: their own messages are still seen, but their attestations count for nothing, so nobody becomes visible on their recommendation alone.

Derived (derived)
  The same format as scores, holding the reputation and trust the author has calculated for every user within N hops. The author calculates them with their own formulas, standard or custom. Any value is accepted, and has no effect on the record's validity, as long as it lies between -1 and +1 and each key is an account ID.

5. MESSAGE RECORDS

The network gives a message's content no meaning. A message follows the common rules and the rules of each field it carries, and nothing more; what it means is for whoever reads it.

6. REACTION RECORDS

A reaction requires target: the record or records reacted to. Any record type may be the target, and multiple record types can be targeted by a single reaction record.

Guideline: a body of the form :<image name>: is a custom emoji, where the image name is the SHA-256 of the image's bytes in hex plus a file extension, as for an avatar. Clients show the image if their server has it.

7. NOTICE RECORDS

A notice requires one additional field, kind: text, 1 to 64 bytes. The kinds below have a meaning to the network. A notice of any other kind is accepted, but the network itself ignores it. It is expected servers will use their own kind definitions to indicate outages, or other information relevant to the server.

Compromised (kind: compromised)
  Says that a key of the account named by id has been compromised. Signed with one of that account's current keys, or posted by one of its adjudicators. Its target names the records its author holds to be fraudulent. The chain does not act on that list: it is evidence for the adjudicators and for readers, so that naming a record cannot be used to take it off the chain.

  Two records are concurrent when neither acknowledges the other, directly or through the records it acknowledges. Two concurrent records from one account conflict when both are key changes or master key changes, whatever either of them says, or when both spend the same output (section 9).

  An account is compromised, as seen by a record, when that record acknowledges, and no later quorum record for the account, any of: a compromised notice for it; two of its own records that conflict; or five of its own records that are all concurrent with one another. An account forking its own history over a key or an output is the same event as a stranger claiming it, and neither can be told from the other, so both are treated as one. Whichever record makes an account compromised is its compromising record: the notice, the later of two conflicting records to reach a reader, or the fifth concurrent record to reach a reader.

  A record from a compromised account takes no effect while the account stays compromised. The conflicting records are the exception: they are what is being decided between. The key change a quorum record names takes effect when it does, and a conflicting spend takes effect as section 9 sets out.

  A record is invalid if it directly acks a record from a compromised account that the account published after, or concurrently with, the compromising record, where that compromising record is in the acking record's own history. Acking such a record indirectly is not affected, or the invalidity would spread through the chain until nothing was left valid. A quorum record may ack the record it names, and is the only record that may: that is how an accepted key change enters the history, so that records acknowledging the quorum see the account's new key.

  Concurrent records that do not conflict are ordinary, up to four at a time. Two clients belonging to one person will sometimes publish at the same moment without either seeing the other, and some records are published on a schedule rather than by a person. Each account is expected to be run by one person or one program at a time, so four is a buffer rather than an allowance: five records none of which saw the others is not somebody on two devices.

Key change (kind: key-change)
  Moves an account to a new working key. Signed with the account's current master key or current working key. The body is the new working key in base64url without padding, and nothing else.

  Where a key change signed with the master key and one signed with the working key disagree, the master key's wins, whichever was published first. The working key is the master key's delegate and cannot overrule it.

Master key change (kind: master-key-change)
  Moves an account to a new master key. Signed with the account's current master key. The body is the new master public key in base64url without padding, and nothing else.

Quorum (kind: quorum)
  Ends an account's compromised state by settling which of its key changes the account's adjudicators accept. Its target names that key change, and its endorse field names the records in which the adjudicators endorsed it. Where the account was compromised without competing key changes, its target instead names the compromised notice, or one of the records that made the account compromised, and the account carries on with the keys it had. Anyone may post one: it decides nothing itself, it only states a count anybody can check.

  It is valid when more than half of the account's adjudicators have endorsed the record it names, counting each adjudicator once. An adjudicator that endorsed more than one of the competing key changes is counted in neither total and not counted in the half. Where no record ever reaches that number the account stays compromised.

  An adjudicator endorses by naming the record in the endorse field of any record it signs. It does not ack that record, which it may not, so endorsing is possible without joining a forked history -- which is why endorse is a field of its own rather than another kind of ack.

8. RELEASE RECORDS

A release record marks the start of a change to the rules, and carries the new rules in its rules field; its body may say what changed. It is the only record whose ack may name records of an older version. Every record in its ack must be of the same previous version, and every record that names the release in its ack must be of the release's version.

9. CURRENCY

Every account is also a currency: its own, named by its account ID, and issued by that account, its issuer. Most accounts never issue any. One that does might be a server's, selling it to pay for what the server provides, or a person keeping track of what they are owed. What a currency is worth and what it buys are its issuer's business; the chain records who holds it and nothing else.

Outputs
  Currency is held in outputs. Each entry in a transfer's out list is one output, of that transfer's currency, belonging to the account in its to. An output is named by the record hash of the record that made it and its index in that record's out list. It is spent whole, by a record from the account it belongs to naming it in in; what the spender means to keep goes back to the spender as a new output, as change.

Issuing
  A transfer of the author's own currency must make at least what it spends, and may make more: that is how currency is issued. An issuer may issue at any time, in any record it signs, including its first identity declaration. Every other transfer must make exactly what it spends: the values in its out list add up to the values of the outputs in its in list.

Validity
  A record carrying a transfer is invalid if its currency names an account whose first identity declaration is not in the record's history; if a to names an account, other than the author's own, whose first identity declaration is not in the record's history; if the values do not add up as above; or if any output in its in list is not in the record's history, belongs to another account, is of another currency, or is already named in the in list of a record in the record's history. So an output can be spent once in any one history, and the only way to spend it twice is in two records neither of which acknowledges the other.

Double spends
  Two such records conflict, and make the account that signed them compromised (section 7). Each may be valid, since neither's history contains the other, and neither takes effect until one is chosen. The currency's issuer chooses, whoever the spender's adjudicators are: it endorses the one it accepts, in the endorse field of any record it signs, and that one takes effect; the other never does. An issuer that endorses more than one of them has chosen none. Where the spender is the issuer itself, its own adjudicators choose instead, by a quorum record naming the spend that takes effect.

  An output made by a record that takes no effect does not exist, and a spend of it takes no effect either.
