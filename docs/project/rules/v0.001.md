ReputableChat rules, version 0.001

These rules define each record, its fields, and when it is valid. This document is the rules field of the genesis record, the developer's first identity declaration. When the rules change, a release record carrying the new rules is added to the chain, and every later record must conform to it. A rule is enforced; a guideline is not. A record that ignores a guideline is still accepted, though clients will likely ignore the nonconforming values.

1. RECORDS

A record is a payload and a signature over it.

Payload
  A JSON object in canonical form: keys sorted, no whitespace, UTF-8, and floating-point numbers written as strings.

Signature
  Ed25519 over the canonical bytes of the payload, by the key in the payload's pubkey or mpubkey field, written as base64url (64 bytes). A record carrying both is valid if the signature verifies against either.

Record hash
  SHA-256 over the canonical payload, a newline, and the signature, written as 64 lowercase hex characters. It is the record's identity. Base64url is written without padding, and neither it nor canonical JSON contains a raw newline.

Text
  UTF-8 with surrounding whitespace removed, and no control characters other than tab, newline and carriage return. Each field sets its own length limit, in bytes.

Decimal
  A number written as a string: an optional minus sign, a whole part of at least one digit with no leading zeros, and optionally a point followed by one to eighteen digits, the last not zero. So "0.5", never ".5" or "0.50"; and "0", never "-0". Each number has exactly one spelling.

File
  A file is too big for the chain, so a record names it by the SHA-256 of its bytes in hex plus an extension of 1 to 5 lowercase letters or digits. The chain checks only that form, not whether the file exists or matches its extension.

Signing key
  An Ed25519 public key written as base64url (32 bytes).

Encryption key
  An X25519 public key written as base64url (32 bytes).

Timestamp
  An integer: whole seconds since 1970-01-01 00:00 UTC.

History
  A record's history is the records its ack names, and their histories, back to the genesis record. It holds a record whether this one's ack names it or a record already in the history does, and it does not hold the record itself. It is fixed when the record is signed, and nothing published afterwards joins it.

  Everywhere these rules say a record is in another's history, that is what they mean. It says nothing about when either was published, only about what was acknowledged.

Validity
  Whether a record is valid is decided by the record and the history it acknowledges, and nothing else. So its author can tell before signing, every reader reaches the same answer, and no later record can make an earlier one invalid.

  An invalid record is refused, and no record may acknowledge it. A valid record joins the chain, and any record may acknowledge it. A valid record may still be:

  Tentative: in effect, but not yet confirmed, and not disputed, such as a key change no quorum has named.
  Disputed: waiting on a decision (sections 7 and 10).
  Confirmed: settled by a quorum (section 7), or for a spend by its currency's issuer (section 10).
  Void: overruled for good. It stays on the chain and does nothing.
  Orphaned: joined too late, so it and the heartbeat that orphaned it cannot both be in one history (section 9). A record is orphaned against that heartbeat rather than in itself, and is perfectly good on the side that holds it.

2. COMMON FIELDS

Each field is required unless marked optional, and some record types require optional ones. Brackets give the kind of value a field holds, as defined in section 1; [Record hashes] is a list of them.

Record type (type) [Text]
  Three or more parts separated by colons: "reputablechat", the record type, which is one of those below, and the rules version the record conforms to. The chain ignores any further parts. Every part after the first is 1 to 32 characters of lowercase letters, digits, dots and hyphens, and the whole is at most 128 bytes. A record must conform to the newest rules it acknowledges; one whose version does not match them is invalid.

  attestation: how reliable the author finds other accounts.
  heartbeat: acks that join many records at once.
  identity: an account's handle and keys.
  message: something to display.
  notice: an alert, which may or may not be displayed.
  reaction: a reaction to other records, such as a like.
  release: a new version of these rules.

  Example: reputablechat:message:v0.001

Account ID (id) [Record hash]
  The record hash of an account's first identity declaration. Required on every record except that declaration itself, which carries no id because its own record hash is the account ID.

Working key (pubkey) [Signing key]
  Optional, but required on every first identity declaration and on any record signed with the working key. A record carries pubkey, mpubkey or both, and is signed with one of them; a record carrying neither is invalid. The working key is the one an account uses day to day.

  An account's keys, as seen by a record, are those set by its first identity declaration, as replaced by the latest change to each of them in the record's history. Latest along the acks, which is the only order there is: a key change names the new key and not the one it replaces, so two changes the history orders have a later one, and two it does not are concurrent and conflict.

  A key change is tentative until a quorum names it, and confirmed from then on. An account's current keys are its last confirmed keys and the key of every tentative change on top of them, including a change a later one has superseded. A record must be signed with one of those keys or it is invalid: a new key works at once rather than waiting on anybody, the last confirmed key cannot be taken from the person whose key was stolen, and a superseded key keeps signing so that whoever held it can say so.

  A record signed with a key that a key change in its own history replaced contests that change, and makes the account disputed (section 7). Replaced means the key that was current before that change, whether the first identity declaration set it or an earlier change did. The last confirmed key and a superseded tentative key both count: a key still signing after it was replaced is its holder saying the replacement was not theirs. A record that does not acknowledge the change contests nothing -- a client that has not caught up is signing with a key it has every reason to think current.

  So a key cannot keep working quietly. Either it acknowledges the change that replaced it, which disputes the account and puts the two in front of the adjudicators, or it acknowledges nothing that holds that change, which is a branch of its own that nothing can join for long (section 9).

  Guideline: an adjudicator asked to settle a contested key change waits to endorse it until it can verify that the account's owner made it.

Ack (ack) [Record hashes]
  The most recent records the author has seen and chooses to acknowledge: sorted, with no duplicates, at most 16, or in a heartbeat as many as fit in 1,048,576 bytes, counted as the canonical bytes of the ack value itself, brackets, quotes and commas included. Empty in the genesis record and nowhere else. Every record named must share this record's version, unless this is a release. Following acks back from any record leads to the genesis record.

Body (body) [Text]
  At most 16,000 bytes, and may be empty. The chain checks only that it is Text, so it may hold plain text or structured data such as a JSON object.

Rules (rules) [Text]
  1 to 64,000 bytes: a complete rules document. Required on the genesis record and every release, and allowed on no other record.

Record timestamp (ts) [Timestamp]
  When the author says they signed. Not verified, except between heartbeats (section 9).

Target (target) [Record hashes]
  Optional. The records this one replies to, comments on or reacts to: sorted, with no duplicates, at most 256.

Endorse (endorse) [Record hashes]
  Optional. The records the author accepts: sorted, with no duplicates, at most 256. Each must be in this record's history. Accepting is the author's own say-so and nothing more -- an adjudicator accepting a key change, an issuer accepting a spend, a quorum accepting the endorsements it counts. The chain acts on it only where these rules say so.

Transfer (transfer) [Object]
  Optional. Moves currency (section 10). It may hold currency, in and out, and must hold in, out or both.

  currency: the account ID of the currency moved. Omitted only when issuing new currency, which is always the author's own: then in is omitted too.
  in: the outputs this record spends, each named by the record hash of the record that made it: sorted, with no duplicates, at most 256. A record makes at most one output per account, so the hash and this record's author name the output. Omitted only when issuing new currency.
  out: the outputs this record makes: 1 to 256, sorted by to, with no two to the same account. Each holds value, a decimal greater than zero, and to, an account ID. Omitted to destroy everything this record spends.

  When both in and out are present, the values in out add up to exactly the values of the outputs in in, or the record is invalid. A transfer without in issues currency; one without out destroys it.

Title (title) [Text]
  Optional. 1 to 280 bytes.

File (file) [File]
  Optional. Files the record refers to: in the author's order, with no duplicates, at most 16.

Url (url) [Text]
  Optional. 1 to 2,048 bytes, with no whitespace. The chain does not check that it is a URL.

Language (lang) [Text]
  Optional. 1 to 35 bytes of letters, digits and hyphens: the language the record is written in.

  Guideline: a BCP 47 language tag, such as "en" or "pt-BR".

Master public key (mpubkey) [Signing key]
  Optional, but required on any record signed with the master key. A first identity declaration that carries it declares the master key, and may be signed with either key. The master key differs from the working key in two things only, and nowhere else in these rules: it alone can change itself (section 7), and a quorum cannot prefer a working key to it (section 7). It is otherwise a key the account signs with, which is why it is worth keeping offline.

3. IDENTITY DECLARATION RECORDS

An identity declaration requires title, which holds the handle, and may carry an avatar, a bio, an encryption key and adjudicators. Each new declaration replaces the account's previous one, except as stated for adjudicators.

Handle (title)
  The name the account goes by: 1 to 64 bytes. Handles are not unique; clients show the start of the account ID beside one.

Avatar (file)
  The first file in the list: an image.

  Guideline: 128 x 128, with a common image extension. Servers will likely ignore images that do not conform.

Bio (body)
  Text about the account.

Encryption key (epubkey) [Encryption key]
  Optional. The chain does not use it, only records which is current: the one on the latest identity declaration in a record's history. A declaration without one says the account has none.

Adjudicators (adjudicators)
  Optional. Account IDs, in the author's order, with no duplicates, at most 16: the accounts that settle this account's disputes, deciding which of its records are confirmed and which void. An absent or empty list names the developer's account alone.

  A declaration with a new list does not change the list by itself: the list last confirmed stays in force until a quorum names the new one. Otherwise any key that can sign for the account could appoint the accounts that decide who owns it.

  Guideline: an adjudicator endorses regularly, so that no account is left disputed for want of an answer, and waits long enough before endorsing a key change that the owner can notice and contest it.

The genesis record is the one identity declaration that carries rules.

4. ATTESTATION RECORDS

An attestation requires scores, and may carry derived.

Scores (scores)
  A map from account IDs to the author's opinion of each: reputation and trust, both decimals from -1 to +1. Reputation is the author's opinion of the account's messages; trust, of its attestations. A trust of 0 means the account's messages are still seen but its attestations count for nothing.

  Guideline: trust is 0 or 1.

Derived (derived)
  The same format as scores: the reputation and trust the author has calculated for accounts further away, by any method. Each key must be an account ID and each value between -1 and +1.

5. MESSAGE RECORDS

A message has no meaning to the chain beyond its fields.

6. REACTION RECORDS

A reaction requires target: the records it reacts to, of any type.

Guideline: a body of the form :<file>: is a custom emoji, where <file> is a File, as for an avatar. Clients show the image if their server has it.

7. NOTICE RECORDS

A notice requires kind [Text], 1 to 64 bytes. The kinds below mean something to the chain; it accepts and ignores any other, which servers may use for outages and the like.

Compromised (kind: compromised)
  Says a key of an account has been compromised, and makes the account disputed. Signed with one of that account's current keys, or by one of its adjudicators. Its target is required and names records of one account and no other, which is how it names the account; a target spanning two accounts names neither and the notice is invalid. What it names is the records its author holds to be fraudulent, or, where there are none, any record of the account such as its first identity declaration. The chain does nothing to the records named.

Disputed
  Two records are concurrent when neither has the other in its history. Two concurrent records of one account conflict when both change the same key, or both spend the same output, or both endorse different spends of the same double spend of the account's own currency (section 10).

  An account is disputed, as seen by a record, when that record's history holds, with no later quorum for the account, any of: a compromised notice for it; two of its records that conflict; five of its records none of which has another in its history; or one of its records that contests a key change, as section 2 sets out under the working key. Disputed says only that the account's records wait on a decision, not whose fault it is.

  While an account is disputed, its records are disputed. A quorum settles the dispute: the record it names is confirmed, every record that conflicted with it is void, as is any record signed with a key from a void key change, and the account's other records are no longer disputed. Which of two conflicting spends is confirmed is for the currency's issuer (section 10).

  Up to four concurrent records of one account that do not conflict are ordinary. An account is expected to be run by one person or program at a time; the four are a buffer for two devices or a scheduled post.

Key change (kind: key-change)
  Moves an account to a new working key. Signed with one of the account's current keys. The body is the new working key in base64url, and nothing else.

Master key change (kind: master-key-change)
  Moves an account to a new master key. Signed with the current master key. The body is the new master public key in base64url, and nothing else.

Quorum (kind: quorum)
  Ends a dispute. Its target names the record the account's adjudicators confirm, and its endorse field names their endorsements of it. Where no key change is in dispute, it names the compromised notice or one of the records that made the account disputed, and the account keeps its keys. Anyone may post one; it only states a count anyone can check.

  It is valid when more than half of the account's adjudicators have endorsed the record it names, counted among the adjudicators left after any that endorsed more than one of the conflicting records, which count for none of them. If no record ever reaches that number, the account stays disputed.

  Where the records that made the account disputed include a key change signed with the master key, or a record signed with a key that such a change replaced, a quorum is valid only if the record it names is signed with the master key or with a key that a master-signed key change introduced. Whether anything conflicts makes no difference, and the keys the first identity declaration set do not count as introduced by the master key.

  So once the owner has moved the key with the master key, nothing the working key can reach is nameable: not the change a thief signed with the stolen key, not a further change signed with the key that one introduced, not an ordinary record or a compromised notice of theirs. The adjudicators may name the master key's change, or a later record signed with a key it introduced, or nothing at all.

  So the master key prevails through the quorum rather than instead of it, and the dispute is still the window: an owner holding only a working key contests, which disputes the account, and then has until the quorum to fetch the master key and publish a change with it for the adjudicators to name. Where no master-signed change replaced the key that signed any of the records in dispute, they may name whichever they judge right. Where the only master-signed change is a thief's and the owner never produces another, they can name nothing they believe, and the account stays disputed rather than changing hands.

  The record it names is confirmed. Where it is a key change, every other change of the same kind that was tentative when the quorum was signed is void, and its key can no longer sign. A change published after the quorum starts tentative like any other.

  An adjudicator endorses a record by naming it in the endorse field of any record it signs.

8. RELEASE RECORDS

A release starts a new version of the rules, which it carries in its rules field; its body may say what changed. It is the only record whose ack may name records of an older version: every record in its ack must be of the previous version, and every record that acks the release must be of the release's version.

9. HEARTBEAT RECORDS

A heartbeat joins records. An ordinary record acknowledges at most 16 others; a heartbeat acknowledges as much of the frontier as it can, so branches rejoin quickly. Besides the fields every record carries, it may hold endorse, and nothing else; its body is empty. Anyone may publish heartbeats; in practice servers will.

  A heartbeat must directly ack its author's previous heartbeat, unless that heartbeat is of an earlier version, or is incompatible with it (see Split). Both exceptions start the publisher a fresh chain of heartbeats; a release would otherwise leave every publisher unable to publish, since the ack rule refuses the old version and this one requires it.

  Its ts must be at least 480 seconds after that of its author's previous heartbeat, where it acks one. A heartbeat that acks none of its author's is the first of a chain and has no such floor.

  Guideline: servers refuse records whose ts is far from their own clock, and a publisher heartbeats about every 10 minutes.

Split
  A record is orphaned by a heartbeat that does not hold it in its history and comes 256 or more heartbeats after the newest heartbeat by the same publisher in the record's history, or after that publisher's first if there is none. A record whose history holds both is invalid.

  This stops back-dating: a record that acknowledges only old heartbeats, to look old, cannot be joined once the publisher's heartbeats are 256 past it. That is about 43 hours at the guideline pace and never less than 34, which is 256 heartbeats at the 480-second floor. The count is against one publisher, so the fastest publisher a record acknowledges is the one that sets its deadline, and a publisher at the floor is within the rules.

  When records are orphaned, the chain has split: each side holds records the other never can, and a record holds one side or the other. The rules do not say which side is right.

10. CURRENCY

Any account may also be a currency, named by its account ID and issued by that account, its issuer. What a currency is worth and buys is its issuer's business; the chain records only who holds it.

Becoming a currency
  An account becomes a currency with an identity declaration, first or later, carrying a transfer that issues new currency. As seen by a record, an account is a currency when such a declaration is in the record's history or is the record.

Outputs
  Currency is held in outputs: each entry in a transfer's out is an output of that transfer's currency, belonging to its to. It is spent whole, by a record of the account it belongs to; what the spender keeps goes back to itself as a new output.

Issuing and destroying
  An issuer holds an unlimited amount of its own currency, and issues it with a transfer that omits in and currency, in any record it signs. Anyone may destroy currency they hold with a transfer that omits out, which destroys everything it spends. Every other transfer passes on exactly what it spends.

Validity
  A record carrying a transfer is invalid if the currency it moves is not a currency as seen by the record; if a to other than the author is not an account in the record's history; if its values do not add up (section 2); or if an output it spends is not in its history, belongs to another account, is of another currency, or is already spent by a record in its history. So an output can be spent twice only by two records neither of which has the other in its history.

Double spends
  A double spend is two such records. Both may be valid; they conflict and make their author's account disputed (section 7).

  Both spends are disputed until the currency's issuer chooses one by endorsing it, in any record it signs; that spend is confirmed and the other void. The issuer chooses for every double spend of its currency, including its own. A quorum that ends the spender's dispute does not choose between the spends.

  A record that endorses both spends is invalid, as is one that endorses a spend when its history already holds the issuer's endorsement of the other. Two concurrent issuer records that endorse different spends conflict (section 7); the endorsement the issuer's quorum names is confirmed, and the other is void.

  An issuer chooses only while its account is not disputed, as seen by the endorsing record; until then the double spend waits.

  An output made by a void record does not exist, and a spend of it is void.
